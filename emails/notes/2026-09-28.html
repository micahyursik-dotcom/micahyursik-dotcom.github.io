this week's note

your brain can't do both

the verse
“Do not be anxious about anything, but in every situation, by prayer and petition, with thanksgiving, present your requests to God. And the peace of God, which transcends all understanding, will guard your hearts and your minds in Christ Jesus.”
— Philippians 4:6–7
the shift

I didn't get my Jesus time this week. Not the kind I wanted, anyway — the quiet-chair-and-coffee kind. By Wednesday I could feel it: the low hum of worry under everything, like a fridge running in another room.

What if that wasn't the whole picture?

Here's the thing I learned that I can't stop thinking about: the part of your brain that gives praise and thanks is the same part that produces anxiety. It can't run both at once. Gratitude and worry are fighting for the same seat.

The lie I'd been agreeing with: that I have to earn peace with a good quiet time, and if I didn't get one, worry is just what the day is going to be. That's not from the Lord.

The truth is right there in Philippians — "with thanksgiving." Paul doesn't say get your life quiet first. He says pray with thanks and the peace shows up and guards you. Guards. Like a wall. I can choose thanks in the car line with a screaming two-year-old and my brain physically has to let go of the worry to do it. That's not a vibe, that's how He built us.

the reach · practice it today

Today, every time the worry loop starts, I'm saying three thank-yous out loud. Not in my head — out loud, even if it's a whisper over the sink. "Thank you for this kitchen. Thank you for her laugh. Thank you that you're here." Then I move on. Three, out loud, every time. Watch what it does to the hum.

a short prayer
Jesus, we didn't get the quiet time and we're choosing you anyway. Thank you. Thank you. Thank you. Take the seat the worry was sitting in and guard us today. Amen.
